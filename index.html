About Matt

The 90 days that taught me leadership is a transfer of energy

In 1999, Capital One gave Matt a mandate: stand up a brand-new internet servicing center in Seattle. Hire 250 people. Launch in 90 days. Other side of the country.

He carried it the way many ambitious leaders carry their first major mandate — pace up, head down, trying to be the person with the answer in every room. Then a teammate named Jim pulled him aside and told him he had a strong team around him — and that they could carry the load if he let them.

That was the moment. Not a strategy moment. An identity moment.

The site launched fully staffed. Within twelve months, Seattle became the highest-performing site in the network.

The deeper lesson lasted longer than the result: under pressure, leadership is not just the transfer of strategy. It is the transfer of energy. Twenty-five years later, that is still the work.

Core Offer — Private 1:1 Coaching

You know how you want to show up. The question is what keeps getting in the way.

1:1 coaching is often a fit when something that helped you succeed is beginning to work against you. Maybe you're carrying too much because letting go feels risky. Overthinking because getting the decision wrong feels unusually consequential. Avoiding a conversation because disappointing someone feels harder than continuing to absorb the cost. Becoming more controlling as the stakes rise — even though you know your team needs more room. Or realizing the success you've been chasing no longer feels quite like the success you want.

The work is deeply personal, but it is practical. We make the pattern specific, examine what may be underneath it, decide how you want to respond instead, and test that choice in real life.

Six conversations. Approximately twelve weeks. Real life in between.

We meet for 60 minutes every other week. That gives us enough proximity to go beneath the issue that brought you in — and enough space between conversations for real life to provide the reps. The work is personalized. There is no fixed curriculum you need to complete. Between sessions, brief text, email, or voice-note access can be used for appropriate calibration around consequential moments without making the coach the answer.

At the end of the six-session engagement, we make an honest decision about what comes next: carry the work forward independently, continue into a longer developmental relationship if there is meaningful value in doing so, or choose another path.

Six sessions gets beneath the issue. What happens next depends on what we discover.

The goal isn't perfect behavior.

It's seeing yourself more clearly. Catching the pattern sooner. Creating more space between what happens and how you respond. Making choices that align more closely with your values and the person you are becoming. Building evidence that you can operate differently when the stakes are real. And increasingly knowing how to do that without me.

Good coaching should make the coach less necessary.

For Organizations

When the problem is no longer just the leader.

Sometimes the gap is not your awareness, your intention, or your capability. It is that the organization around you cannot consistently hold the standard under pressure. Priorities blur. Ownership narrows to a few people. Hard conversations happen too late. Too much still depends on the leader to carry the load.

The result is not a lack of effort. It is a system that cannot sustain what you are asking of it.
