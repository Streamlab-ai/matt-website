What got you here may be starting to get in the way of what comes next.

You've built a career on being capable. Solving problems. Carrying responsibility. Getting results.

But maybe you're overthinking decisions you used to make quickly. Carrying more than you should. Reacting in ways you don't like. Or realizing that the way you've learned to succeed is beginning to cost more than you're willing to pay.

You may not need more advice. You may need a place to understand why the same patterns keep showing up — and what you want to do differently when they do.

Recognition

The gap is rarely in what you know.

You may already know how to delegate — but take the work back when the outcome really matters.

You know how to listen — but become defensive when your judgment is questioned.

You know the conversation you need to have — but keep waiting for a better time.

You know you don't need to carry everything — but being needed has become difficult to separate from being valuable.

The issue may not be capability.

It may be the interference that gets in the way of accessing that capability when it matters most.

W. Timothy Gallwey captured this with a simple equation:

Performance = Potential − Interference

Over the course of my career, I came to see that most of the biggest performance gains didn't come from adding more potential.

They came from reducing the interference already sitting between capable people and the results they were capable of producing.

The Ripple Effect

Significant change follows a sequence.

01

Awareness

See what is happening — and get curious about the emotion, thoughts, stories, or beliefs underneath it.

02

Alignment

Separate what happened from the story you built around it and decide how you actually want to show up.

03

Activation

Choose a specific response and practice it when real life provides the friction.

04

Amplification

What changes in you changes what others experience. What others experience repeatedly begins to shape culture.

Awareness. Alignment. Activation. Amplification.

I think of this as the Ripple Effect — because what changes in you rarely stops with you.

About Matt

This work didn't start with coaching.

For more than three decades, I worked inside fast-growing organizations where the challenge was rarely just having the right strategy.

It was helping people, teams, and systems keep up with growth without adding unnecessary complexity, cost, or dependence on a few people.

One of the earliest lessons came at Capital One, where I was asked to build and launch a new servicing operation in Seattle in 90 days.

I entered that assignment believing leadership meant carrying more of the load.

I left it understanding something very different:

Leadership is a transfer of energy.

What is happening inside us doesn't stay inside us.

It shows up in our decisions, conversations, presence, and eventually in the people around us.

That lesson followed me through executive roles at Corpay/FLEETCOR, Credit One Bank, and PREMIER Bankcard and ultimately into the work I do today.

Private 1:1 Executive Coaching

You know how you want to show up. The question is what keeps getting in the way.

1:1 coaching is often a fit when something that helped you succeed is beginning to work against you.

Maybe you're carrying too much because letting go feels risky. Overthinking because getting the decision wrong feels unusually consequential. Avoiding a conversation because disappointing someone feels harder than continuing to absorb the cost. Or becoming more controlling as the stakes rise — even though you know the people around you need more room.

Or maybe you're realizing that the success you've been chasing no longer feels quite like the success you want.

The work is deeply personal, but it is practical.

We make the pattern specific, get curious about what may be underneath it, decide how you want to respond instead, and test that choice in real life.

For Organizations

When the problem is no longer just the leader.

Sometimes the gap is not your awareness, intention, or capability.

It is that the organization around you cannot consistently hold the standard under pressure.

Priorities blur. Ownership narrows to a few people. Hard conversations happen too late. Too much still depends on the leader to carry the load.

The result is not a lack of effort.

It is a system that cannot sustain what you are asking of it.

Elevate You was built for this moment — when the work needs to move from the individual to the organization, and behavior needs to hold without constant reinforcement.
