Private Executive Coaching with Matt Krall

1:1 coaching for senior executives and operators

You know how you want to show up. The question is what keeps getting in the way.

You have probably spent years becoming good at what you do.

You know how to make decisions. Have difficult conversations. Delegate. Create clarity. Carry responsibility.

But knowing what to do and being able to do it when the stakes are real are not always the same thing.

Maybe you're carrying too much because letting go feels risky.

Overthinking because getting the decision wrong feels unusually consequential.

Avoiding a conversation because disappointing someone feels harder than continuing to absorb the cost.

Becoming more controlling as the stakes rise — even though you know the people around you need more room.

Or realizing that the success you've been chasing no longer feels quite like the success you want.

Something that helped you succeed may be beginning to work against you.

That is often where our work begins.

Start with the Identity Audit

15-minute guided self-audit. No obligation to continue into coaching.

A room with no agenda. Specific truth. Real-world testing.

The higher you go, the fewer rooms there are where you don't have to be anything for anyone.

This is one of them.

No board to reassure. No team waiting for an answer. No family member affected by the decision. No coach arriving with a predetermined conclusion.

I don't arrive with an agenda for who you need to become.

I listen before prescribing.

I challenge your thinking without needing you to arrive at my conclusion.

And we reduce the complexity until we can see the moment clearly enough to work with it.

The work is deeply personal, but it is practical.

The goal is not simply to understand yourself better.

It is to take what you discover back into the meeting, decision, relationship, or difficult moment where the pattern actually matters.

The session creates clarity. Life provides the proof.

How the work happens

01 — Make it specific.

Vague friction is difficult to change.

We get specific about the moment.

What happened?

What did you notice?

What were you feeling?

What did you tell yourself?

What did you do next?

Rather than talking generally about being “more confident,” “a better communicator,” or “less stressed,” we work with something real enough to examine.

A conversation you avoided.

A decision you cannot seem to make.

A moment when you took the work back.

A reaction that did not reflect how you wanted to show up.

Specificity gives us something we can actually work with.

02 — Go upstream.

The behavior we see is rarely the whole story.

We get curious about what may be underneath it — the thoughts, stories, beliefs, expectations, or identities that made the response make sense in the moment.

Not to judge them.

To understand them.

Sometimes the belief that helped you succeed earlier in your career is exactly the belief creating friction now.

If I don't carry it, it may not get done.

If I disappoint someone, I have failed them.

If I don't have the answer, I may lose credibility.

If I slow down, I may fall behind.

Once we can see what is shaping the pattern, we can ask a more useful question:

Does this still reflect who I want to be and how I want to show up?

03 — Test it in real life.

Insight isn't the finish line.

You decide what you want to do differently when the moment returns.

Maybe that means asking one question before defending your position.

Having the conversation instead of waiting another week.

Letting someone else own the decision rather than taking it back.

Saying no without over-explaining.

Pausing long enough to separate what happened from the story you immediately built around it.

Then real life provides the rep.

We look at what happened, what you learned, and what needs to be practiced next.

That is how awareness begins becoming capacity.

How I coach

How I coach matters as much as what I coach.

I don't believe my job is to hand you a predetermined model of who you should become.

My job is to help you see more clearly.

To listen carefully enough that we can reduce complexity to what matters most.

To challenge your thinking without replacing it with mine.

To help you distinguish between what actually happened and the story, belief, or expectation you may have attached to it.

And then to translate what you discover into something specific enough to test in your actual life.

I am less interested in creating dependence than I am in helping you develop the ability to navigate these moments increasingly well on your own.

Good coaching should make the coach less necessary.

Six conversations. Approximately twelve weeks. Real life in between.

We meet for 60 minutes every other week.

That gives us enough proximity to go beneath the issue that brought you in — and enough space between conversations for real life to provide the reps.

There is no fixed curriculum you need to complete.

The work follows what is actually happening.

Between sessions, brief text, email, or voice-note access can be used for appropriate calibration around consequential moments without making the coach the answer.

At the end of the six-session engagement, we make an honest decision about what comes next.

You may carry the work forward independently.

We may decide there is meaningful value in continuing into a longer developmental relationship.

Or another path may make more sense.

Six sessions gets beneath the issue. What happens next depends on what we discover.

The goal isn't perfect behavior.

Progress does not mean you never get defensive again.

Never overthink.

Never take back control.

Never avoid something uncomfortable.

It means you begin seeing yourself more clearly.

You catch the pattern sooner.

You create more space between what happens and how you respond.

Your choices begin aligning more closely with your values and the person you are becoming.

You build evidence that you can respond differently when the stakes are real.

And increasingly, you know how to do that without me.

The perspective I bring to the room

My coaching perspective was shaped long before I became a coach.

For more than three decades, I worked inside fast-growing organizations where decisions had consequences, pressure was real, and performance depended on more than knowing the right strategy.

I served as President of PREMIER Bankcard, Chief Operating Officer of Credit One Bank, and Senior Vice President of Operations at Corpay/FLEETCOR, with earlier roles at GE Retail Consumer Finance and Capital One.

Those experiences taught me that performance problems are not always knowledge problems.

Often, the real challenge is the interference between what capable people know and what they can consistently access when pressure is live.

That is the lens I bring into coaching today.

Start with the Identity Audit

You don't need to know exactly what you want to work on.

The Identity Audit is a 15-minute guided exercise designed to help you get more specific about the person you want to become, the pattern that keeps showing up, and what that pattern may be costing.

It is not a diagnostic or psychometric assessment.

It is simply a useful place to begin.

Start the Identity Audit

What happens after the Audit?

If a conversation would be useful, you can schedule a complimentary 30-Minute Clarity Conversation.

We'll look at what stood out, make one area of friction more specific, explore what may be underneath it, and identify one or two things you can begin testing in real life.

There is no obligation to continue into coaching.

Sometimes the right next step is 1:1 work.

Sometimes it is another form of support.

Sometimes it is simply taking what you learned and working with it yourself.
